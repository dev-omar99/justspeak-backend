# CEFR fit check for the curriculum programmes (draft)

Status: a first check by the author of the scripts (JustSpeak). It is not a teacher's review. Each programme still needs a human English-teacher read before any lesson goes live.

## What the check uses
Sources actually opened (Council of Europe, public pages):
- Common reference levels, global scale: https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/table-1-cefr-3.3-common-reference-levels-global-scale
- Level descriptions: https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/level-descriptions
- CEFR Companion Volume (2020), opening part only: https://rm.coe.int/cefr-companion-volume-with-new-descriptors-2020/16809ea0d4
- Cambridge A2 Key / B1 Preliminary format page (exam format only): https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/exams-and-tests/qualifications/preliminary/format/

Not opened: the full descriptor tables of the Companion Volume and the English Vocabulary Profile (the page did not load). So the check is against the global scale only.

## Rule used for each level
- A1: familiar everyday expressions, introduce self, ask and answer simple personal questions. Lessons: one-step tasks, single words or short phrases are enough.
- A2: sentences about immediate needs, simple exchanges on familiar matters. Lessons: short routine tasks (orders, directions, small problems).
- B1: deal with most situations while travelling, describe experiences, give reasons and opinions. Lessons: explain, compare, complain politely, tell a story.
- B2: interact fluently with native speakers, explain a view with pros and cons. Lessons: discovery, negotiation, objections, formal complaints.
- C1: flexible, effective use for social, academic and professional purposes; structure and cohesion. Lessons: executive presentations, reframing, complex negotiation, subtle tone.

## Result of the scripted checks (all programmes)
- Every level has 4 lessons per unit. Goals are short (about 6 to 10 words on average); A1 goals have no word over 10 letters.
- Rubric hint includes a level note, so scoring is lenient at A1 and strict at C1.
- Known weak spots to review by a teacher: some A2 lessons ask for past tense or comparison (A2 allows simple past; comparison is borderline); C1 lessons in Everyday Life and Travel lean on B2 skills with a more formal setting. Check the first two units of each C1 level.
- The CEFR descriptors give level and scale only. The lesson topics and wording are ours (JustSpeak-owned), not copied from any course book or word list.
